**Spec: Why does Gauss's law work for any closed surface?**

1. Surface: a deformable bubble around a point charge, with field lines drawn.
2. Count: lines leaving the bubble stay constant however it's squashed, giving q/ε₀.
3. Why: area grows as r² while the field falls as 1/r², so each solid-angle wedge carries the same flux.
4. Outside charge: a charge outside sends in as many lines as it takes out, so the net is 0.

**Spec: Why do bulbs light instantly when electrons drift at mm/s?**

1. Pipe of marbles: push one end and the far marble moves at once.
2. Wire: zoom on electrons with a random thermal jitter (~10⁵ m/s) plus a tiny drift; the drift speed reads ~1 mm/s.
3. Signal: the field travels at nearly light speed and switches every electron on together.
4. Compare: walking pace against the field's speed on a log scale.

**Spec: Why Biot–Savart? Each piece of wire adds its bit**

1. Element: drag a small segment I dl and see its dB arrow at a test point.
2. Rules: dB ∝ 1/r², and is perpendicular to both dl and r̂ (right hand).
3. Sum: add all the segments of a loop; the sideways parts cancel.
4. Results: wire, loop and solenoid fields fall out of the same sum.

**Spec: Why is Lenz's law energy conservation?**

1. Drop: a magnet falls through a coil; the induced current repels it and the fall slows.
2. Flip: reverse the current's sign and the magnet accelerates, its energy growing forever.
3. Ledger: the slowing magnet's lost KE equals the heat in the coil.
4. Rule: the induced current always opposes the change in flux.

**Spec: Why does a depletion layer form at a p-n junction?**

1. Join: a p-region full of holes meets an n-region full of electrons; carriers start to diffuse across.
2. Cores: each crossing leaves behind an immobile ion core (− on the p side, + on the n side), and the depletion layer grows.
3. Balance: the cores' field drives a drift current back; the layer stops growing when drift equals diffusion, with a barrier of about 0.7 V for Si.
4. Bias slider: forward bias narrows the layer and current flows; reverse bias widens it and the current is only μA.
