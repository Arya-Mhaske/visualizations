{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Guess how a pendulum's period depends on its length, on g and on its mass. Let the dimensions decide.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The period is a time. So lˣ gʸ mᶻ must be a time too: mass appears only in m, so z must be 0 to leave no [M]. Then [L] gives x + y = 0 and [T] gives −2y = 1. So y = −½ and x = ½: T = k √(l/g), and the mass is forced out. A heavier bob has no effect, because nothing else in the problem has a mass to compare it with.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Check the last row: do two pendulums of different length and mass give the same k?
  • Try z = 0.5 with the best x and y. What goes wrong?

    Note Dimensional analysis cannot give the number k. The virtual pendulums measure it, for small swings, as 2π ≈ 6.283. It also assumes that l, g and m are all that matter. If the bob were hung in oil, the formula would need more variables.
