{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Try sin(5 m). Then try the sine of an angle. See what goes wrong with the first.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A function like sin x is built from powers of x: x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − … If x is in metres, the terms are in m, m³, m⁵, and these cannot be added. So sin of a length is meaningless. The same goes for log and eˣ, which also have power series. An angle works because it is a ratio: arc ÷ radius is metres divided by metres, a pure number. Its "radian" is a name, not a unit.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Choose "Arc ÷ radius". Why is every term now a pure number?
  • Double the radius and the arc together. Does the angle change?

    Note Only the first three terms are shown. The full series carries on in the same way, with terms in m⁷, m⁹ and so on. Likewise, ln(5 m) is meaningless but ln(5 m ÷ 2 m) is fine.
