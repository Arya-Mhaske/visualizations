See why the area under the left side equals the change in G(y).{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}If G′(y) y′ is the derivative of G(y(x)) by the chain rule, then integrating it over x from a to b must give G(y(b)) − G(y(a)). So "multiply by dx and integrate both sides" is the chain rule run backwards, and dy/dx never has to be a true fraction.{% endblock %} {% block notes %}
Note In the first equation y is positive, so ln and y²/2 are safe. For y′/y = 1 the solution y = C e^x stays positive for C > 0.
