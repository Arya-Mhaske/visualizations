{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Light reflects from the front and the back of a soap film. The two reflections interfere, and the result depends on the colour and on the thickness.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The back reflection travels an extra 2nt through the film. The front reflection also flips its phase, so reflection is bright when 2nt is a half number of wavelengths. Different colours have different wavelengths, so at one thickness one colour is bright and another is cancelled. That is the colour you see.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Find a thickness where green is strongest. What is 2nt there, and how does it compare with half of 550 nm?
  • Thicken to 800 nm. Why do the colours get harder to tell apart?

    Note This goes beyond the NCERT text. It treats light as three colours (650, 550 and 450 nm) at normal incidence, with n = 1.33, so it only shows the idea. Real films reflect every wavelength, and light entering at an angle shifts the colours.
