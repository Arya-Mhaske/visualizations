{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block stage_class %}viz-stage--tall{% endblock %} {% block lead %}Turn a prism slowly and watch how far it bends the ray. The bend does not just rise or fall: it dips.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}D = i + e − A. Turning the prism raises i, which makes the ray enter more slanted, but it also lowers e at the other face. The two changes compete. At one angle they balance: the ray passes symmetrically, with i = e and the ray inside parallel to the base. That is the lowest point of D. On either side the ray is bent more.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Drag the point along the curve. Where is the ray inside parallel to the base?
  • At that lowest point, compare i and e. What do you notice?
  • Raise n. Does the minimum deviation go up or down?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The green dot marks the exact minimum, D = 2 sin⁻¹(n sin A/2) − A. For small i the ray cannot leave the second face, and the curve stops. The same ray is traced at both faces with Snell's law.
