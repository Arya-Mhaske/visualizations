{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Trace two rays from a coin on the bottom of a pool, and see where your eye thinks the coin is.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A ray leaving the water bends away from the normal, so it looks as if it came from higher up. The eye follows the ray back in a straight line and places the coin where that line meets the vertical through the coin. For rays close to vertical this works out to d/n: the water seems 1.33 times shallower.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Set the viewing angle to 0°. Is the apparent depth d/n?
  • Raise the viewing angle. Does the pool look even shallower, or less shallow?
  • Change n to 1.5. What happens to the apparent depth?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The usual formula, apparent depth = real depth ÷ n, holds for rays near the vertical. For a slanted view the coin seems to sit on the vertical through the coin, at depth d tan r / tan i. A real eye sees the rays through a small pupil, so the picture is only approximate at large angles.
