{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Drag the object along the axis. Any two rays from its tip fix the image, and the mirror equation gives the same answer.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A ray that is easy to predict needs no calculation: parallel rays leave through F, rays through F leave parallel, and rays through C come straight back. Solid lines meet at a real image. Dashed lines only seem to meet, which gives a virtual image.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Walk the object from far away to P on the concave mirror. Where does the image turn from real to virtual?
  • Put the object exactly at C, then at F. What happens each time?
  • Turn off one ray. Do the other two still find the image?
  • On the convex mirror, can you ever get a real image?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The mirror is drawn much flatter than its true curve, and the rays turn at the plane through P. This is the paraxial picture the equation assumes.
