{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A short-sighted eye focuses too soon, a long-sighted eye too late. Find the lens that puts the focus back on the retina.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Short sight: the eye is too strong, so the far point is close, and the picture of a distant star forms in front of the retina. A diverging lens spreads the light first. Its power should be P = −1/(far point). Long sight: the eye cannot bend light enough for close objects, so the picture forms behind the retina. A converging lens helps, with P = 1/0.25 − 1/(near point) so that a book at 25 cm looks as if it is at the near point.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Reduce the far point to 0.3 m, then raise it to 2 m. How does the power of the lens change?
  • Long sight with a near point of 1 m. What power gives a clear book at 25 cm?

    Note The eye is a single thin lens 2.27 cm from the retina, with a relaxed power of about 44 D. The spectacles are treated as touching the eye lens, which is close enough for powers of a few dioptres. Real eyes also have astigmatism and age-related changes that are not modelled here.
