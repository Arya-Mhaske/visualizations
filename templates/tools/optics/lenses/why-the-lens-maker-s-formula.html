{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A lens is two curved surfaces back to back. Follow a beam through the first face, then the second, and see where the formula comes from.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The first face alone would focus a parallel beam at v₁ = nR₁/(n − 1). That point becomes the object for the second face. Applying the surface formula again gives 1/v₂ = (n − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂). That v₂ is the focal length of the lens, so 1/f = (n − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂): two refractions, one after the other.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Take R₁ = 5 and R₂ = −5. Compare the "face 1 alone" mark with the final focus.
  • Make the second face flat. Does f match nR₁/(n − 1)?
  • Flip the signs of both radii. What happens to f?

    Note Rays are traced exactly through two spheres. The formula is for a thin lens with rays close to the axis, so the lens is drawn only 0.1 thick and the outer rays cross a little short of F. Signs: a face is positive if its centre is on the right.
