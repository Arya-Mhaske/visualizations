{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Put two thin lenses together and trace a beam through both. Find the single lens that does the same job.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A thin lens bends a ray by an amount proportional to its height: the turn is y/f = yP. With two lenses in contact the ray has almost the same height at both, so the two turns simply add: y(P₁ + P₂). A single lens with power P = P₁ + P₂ would give the same bend. Adding powers is adding bends, and power is measured in dioptres (D = 1/m).{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Set P₁ = 2 D and P₂ = 3 D. What is the focal length of the pair, and is it larger or smaller than either?
  • Set P₂ = −P₁. What do the rays do?
  • Make the second lens diverging, say −2 D. How does the focus move?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note Heights are drawn ten times larger than they are so that the bends can be seen. Thin lenses and rays close to the axis are assumed, and the lenses are 2 cm apart, small compared with the focal lengths. Spectacle wearers know this rule: the power of a lens is quoted in dioptres.
