{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A star is too far away to look any bigger. See what a telescope does instead.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The objective makes a tiny image at its focus, of height f₀ tan α. The eyepiece, placed one fₑ beyond that image, turns the rays from each point into a parallel beam, and the image is seen at the angle β where tan β = f₀ tan α / fₑ. So the angle grows by f₀/fₑ. The image is not larger than the object, and it is not nearer. It just takes up a bigger angle of your view.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Shorten the eyepiece's focal length. What happens to the angle the rays leave at?
  • Compare β ÷ α with f₀/fₑ.
  • Look at the image at the middle. Is it bigger or smaller than the objective lens?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note Angles are exaggerated (up to 8°) so that they can be seen. For real stars α is a tiny fraction of a degree. The image is upside down, as it is in an astronomical telescope. The objective's focal length is fixed at 10 units.
