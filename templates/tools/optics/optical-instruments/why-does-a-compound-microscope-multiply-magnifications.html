{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A microscope has two lenses. See how the second one works on the picture made by the first.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The objective makes a real, enlarged image of the object: that is magnification m₀. The eyepiece does not look at the object at all. It acts as a magnifier on that image and enlarges it by a further factor Mₑ. A picture enlarged by m₀ and then by Mₑ has been enlarged by m₀ × Mₑ, so the magnifications multiply.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Increase the distance between the lenses. Which magnification changes?
  • Shorten the eyepiece's focal length. Which magnification changes?
  • Switch the final image to the near point. How much does Mₑ change?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The bars use a log scale, so that equal steps on the page are equal multiplications. The objective's image is placed so that the eyepiece forms the final image where you choose. The formula m₀ ≈ L/f₀ is the usual approximation when the tube is long; here m₀ is worked out exactly.
