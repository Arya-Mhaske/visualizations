How much light gets through?{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The polaroid passes only the part of the electric field that lies along its axis. Projecting E₀ onto the axis gives E₀ cos θ. The brightness of light goes as the square of its field, so the intensity is I = I₀ cos² θ. A cos θ appears for the amplitude and a second one when it is squared.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Compare the solid curve (intensity) with the dashed one (amplitude). Which is lower?
  • Set θ = 180°. Why is the light as bright as at 0°?

    Note This is Malus's law, for light that is already polarised. Unpolarised light through one polaroid loses half its intensity. The dashed curve is the amplitude, E/E₀ = cos θ, shown to 90°. Drag the point on the graph to turn the polaroid.
