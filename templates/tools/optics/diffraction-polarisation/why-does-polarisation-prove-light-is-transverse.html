{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Put two polaroid sheets in a beam and turn one. Light fades to nothing. Sound never does. Find out why.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A polaroid lets through only vibrations along its axis. That only makes sense if light vibrates sideways, across the beam, so that "along the axis" picks out a direction. Two sheets at 90° leave no vibration that both accept, so the beam stops. A sound wave vibrates along the beam, so there is no sideways direction to select, and no slot can cut it. That light can be polarised proves that it is a transverse wave.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Slowly turn the angle from 0° to 90°. When does the light vanish?
  • Switch to sound. What does the angle do now?
  • Look at the first polaroid. Why does it take away half the unpolarised light?

    Note The views look end-on along the beam. Unpolarised light has vibrations in every sideways direction (eight are drawn). Historically, polarisation (Malus, Young, Fresnel, early 1800s) was what showed light waves are transverse.
