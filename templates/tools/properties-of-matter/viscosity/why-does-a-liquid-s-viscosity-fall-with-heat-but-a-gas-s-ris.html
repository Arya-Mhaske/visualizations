Both curves are scaled to 1 at 293 K (20 °C).{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}In a liquid, layers drag because molecules stick to each other; heat shakes them free, so η falls. In a gas, molecules are far apart and layers drag only when fast molecules swap layers; heat makes them faster, so more momentum is carried across and η rises.{% endblock %} {% block notes %}

Both curves are simple models (an eE/kT escape rule for the liquid, √T for the gas), scaled to 1 at 293 K. They show the trends, not exact data.
