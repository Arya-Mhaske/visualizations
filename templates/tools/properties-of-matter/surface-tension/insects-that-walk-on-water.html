{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A water strider rests on six legs. The surface can pull up with S times the length of its contact line. Is that enough for its weight?{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Each leg dents the surface, and the surface pulls back along the whole edge of the dent, on both sides. The most it can hold is about S × 2 × (leg length) × (number of legs). If the weight is bigger, the insect breaks through, and detergent halves that limit.{% endblock %} {% block notes %}

This is a simple upper bound that treats the contact line as pulling straight up. Real striders also use the hairs on their legs, which hold air.
