{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}The "equal transit time" story says air over the top of a wing must arrive at the same time as air underneath. Test it.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Fast air does mean low pressure along a streamline, but the equal-transit story is wrong: air over the top gets there well ahead of the air below. A flat plate or a symmetric wing tilted at an angle still lifts, because it turns the air downward, and by Newton's third law the air pushes the wing up. Bernoulli and Newton are two views of the same lift.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Set the angle to zero. What is the lift?
  • Double the speed. What happens to the lift?
