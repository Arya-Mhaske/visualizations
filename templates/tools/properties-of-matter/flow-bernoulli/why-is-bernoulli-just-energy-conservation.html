{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Follow a parcel of fluid through a pipe that narrows and rises. The work done on it by the pressure equals its gain of kinetic and potential energy.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Pressure pushes the parcel in at one end, doing P₁A₁v₁dt of work, and the fluid ahead pushes back at the other end, taking away P₂A₂v₂dt. The difference equals the gain in ½mv² + mgh. Dividing by the volume, P + ½ρv² + ρgh is the same at both points: Bernoulli's equation is the work–energy theorem for a fluid.{% endblock %} {% block notes %}

Bernoulli's equation holds for a steady, smooth flow of a fluid with negligible viscosity. Viscosity takes energy out of the flow, so real pressure drops are larger.

{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Narrow the pipe. Where does the pressure go?
  • Raise the outlet. What changes?
  • Check that the three terms add to the same total at both points.
