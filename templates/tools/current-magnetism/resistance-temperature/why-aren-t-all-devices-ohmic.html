{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Sweep the voltage across three devices and watch each one draw its own V–I graph.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Ohm's law is not a rule that every device must follow. It says R stays fixed as V changes, which makes the graph a straight line through the origin. A resistor does that. A lamp's filament heats up, so its R rises and its graph bends. A diode lets current through one way only, so its graph is curved and lopsided.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Sweep the resistor from −1 V to 0.8 V. How does the dashed line change?
  • Do the same for the lamp. Why does R grow as the current grows?
  • Sweep the diode. What happens to the current at −0.5 V and at +0.5 V?
