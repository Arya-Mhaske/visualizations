{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A metal's resistance climbs almost in a straight line with temperature. Use the line to find R at a new temperature, or to find α from two readings.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Between two readings R changes by R₀ α ΔT, so the graph is a straight line of slope R₀α. Knowing α gives the new R. Knowing two readings gives the slope, and dividing by R₀ gives α.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Set R₀ = 100 Ω, α = 4 ×10⁻³ /K and T = 100 °C. What is R? Check it by hand.
  • Switch to "α from 2 readings" and raise the second reading. Does α go up with the slope?
  • Make the second reading smaller than R₀. What does a negative α suggest about the material?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The straight-line rule is a good approximation over a modest range. Over a wide range real metals curve. The reference temperature is 20 °C.
