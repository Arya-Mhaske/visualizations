{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Heat both materials. In each one, two things change: how often electrons collide, and how many carriers there are to carry the current.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Resistance goes as 1/(nτ). Heat makes the ions shake harder, so collisions come sooner and τ falls in both materials. In a metal n is fixed, so R rises. In a semiconductor heat also frees many more carriers, and that gain in n beats the loss in τ, so R falls.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Do the same for the semiconductor. Which bar wins, n or τ?
  • Cool both to 250 K. What happens to the semiconductor's resistance?
