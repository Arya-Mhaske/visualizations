{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A galvanometer can only take 2 mA. Switch on the shunt or the series resistor and see how it can measure amperes or volts.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A small resistance S in parallel (a shunt) takes almost all of a large current, and the coil gets only its safe 2 mA. That makes an ammeter, with a very small resistance, so it disturbs the circuit very little. A large resistance R in series with the coil soaks up almost all of a large voltage, so the coil sees only the share that gives 2 mA. That makes a voltmeter, with a very large resistance.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • With the ammeter and no shunt, set the quantity to 0.6 A. What happens to the needle? Then turn the shunt on.
  • Look at the bar. How much of the current does the coil get?
  • Switch to the voltmeter and raise the range. Does the added resistance go up or down?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The coil is 50 Ω and deflects fully at 2 mA. The shunt and series resistor are chosen so that the range you pick gives full-scale deflection. The same slider values are read as amperes for the ammeter and volts for the voltmeter.
