{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Every tiny piece of a current makes its own bit of field. Look at one piece, then watch many of them add up to a loop and to a straight wire.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}One piece of current, I dl, gives dB = (μ₀/4π) I dl sin θ ÷ r². It is weaker as 1 ÷ r², zero along the wire, and strongest at the sides, pointing square to both the piece and the line to P. Add the pieces of a loop or a wire and their bits make the whole field.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • In One piece, drag P along the wire's own line. What happens to the field?
  • Double the distance from the piece along the same direction. How much weaker is dB?
  • Move P to the other side of the wire. Which way does the field point now?
  • In A loop, put P at the centre and raise the number of pieces. Does the total change?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note A single piece of current cannot flow alone, since charge would pile up at its ends. It is always a piece of a complete circuit. The wire here is 60 cm long, so its field is the field of a very long wire only close to it.
