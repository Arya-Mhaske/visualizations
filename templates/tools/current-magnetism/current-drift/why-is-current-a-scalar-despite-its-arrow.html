{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Current is drawn with an arrow, so is it a vector? Join two wires, swing them to any angle, and count the charge coming out.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Current counts charge passing per second, and charge is just a number. Whatever arrives at a junction must leave, because charge does not pile up there. So currents add as plain numbers, whatever the angle. The arrow only tells which way along the wire the charge goes. It is not a direction in space, because the wire can bend and the current stays the same.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Swing the wire ends around. Does the out wire's current change? Does the vector sum?
  • Bring the two wires to 180°, one above and one below. What would the vector sum say for 3 A and 3 A?
  • Watch Charge in and Charge out. Why does one lag the other, and by a steady amount?
