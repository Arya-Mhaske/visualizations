{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Lay the field lines of a current-carrying solenoid over those of a bar magnet of the same size and strength.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Outside, the two patterns are almost the same: lines leave the N end, curve round and return to the S end. A solenoid is just a stack of current loops, and each loop is a small magnet. Inside a bar magnet, the atoms' tiny current loops add up in the same way. A bar magnet is a solenoid made by the atoms themselves.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Drag the probe far from the magnet. What happens to the angle between the two fields?
  • Put the probe close to a pole. Is the match as good?

The magnet's lines are only drawn outside, where the model is good. Inside a real magnet they run on from S to N, as in the solenoid.
