{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Draw a closed surface round a magnet and count the field lines going out and coming in. Do the same for an electric charge.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Every magnetic field line is a closed loop with no start and no end. A line that leaves a closed surface must come back in somewhere else, so the net flux is exactly zero, wherever the surface is. A lone electric charge is different: its lines begin on it, so a surface round it has net outward flux. That is Gauss's law for magnetism: ∮B·dA = 0.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • With the magnet, slide the surface across it. Does the net ever leave zero?
  • Switch to the point charge. Put the surface round it, then slide it away. What changes?
  • Shrink the surface so it sits wholly inside the magnet. What is the flux in and out now?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The magnet is a uniformly magnetised ball, and its field is the exact one: uniform inside and a dipole outside. The surface is a sphere, shown here as a circle through its axis. The "lines" are the flux through the surface, in units where one charge sends out 1. Drag the dot, or use the slider, to move the surface.
