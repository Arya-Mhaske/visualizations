{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block stage_class %}viz-stage--tall{% endblock %} {% block lead %}Fire a charge at an angle to B. Split its velocity into a part along B and a part across B, and see what each part does.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The part of v across B is turned into a circle, as before. The part along B feels no force, because v × B is zero for it, so it carries on at a steady speed. A circle plus a steady drift along its axis is a helix. Its radius depends on v sin θ, its pitch on v cos θ.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Switch to the view from above. What shape do you see, and which part of v makes it?
  • Switch to the side view. How far does the charge move along B in each turn?
  • Set θ = 90°, then lower it. When does the pitch disappear, and when does the radius?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note Units are chosen so charge and mass are both 1. Changing v or B scales the whole picture but not its shape: only θ changes the shape. The angled view is a simple oblique drawing. Only the last two turns are drawn.
