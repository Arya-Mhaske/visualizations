{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Send the same charge through a magnetic field and an electric field. One of them speeds it up. The other does not.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The magnetic force is F = qv × B, and a cross product is always perpendicular to v. A force at right angles to the motion has no part along the motion, so F·v = 0 and the speed cannot change. It can only turn the charge. An electric force has a part along v, so it can do work and raise the speed.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • In the magnetic field, press Play. Does the velocity arrow ever grow? Does it turn?
  • Look at the force arrow. What angle does it make with the velocity at every moment?
  • Switch to the electric field. Watch the speed and the kinetic energy. What is different?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note Units are chosen so that the charge and mass are both 1. The charge is positive, B points out of the page, and E points to the right. A positive charge goes clockwise in B out of the page.
