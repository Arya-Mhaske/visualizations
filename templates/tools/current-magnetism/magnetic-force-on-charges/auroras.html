{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block stage_class %}viz-stage--tall{% endblock %} {% block lead %}Charged particles from the Sun spiral along Earth's field lines. Some bounce back. Some reach the air and make an aurora.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Near the poles the field lines crowd together, so B is stronger. The sideways speed grows as B grows, and since the speed stays fixed, the speed along the field shrinks. If it reaches zero before the atmosphere, the charge turns back. A charge fired more nearly along the field gets through and hits the air, which glows. That is an aurora.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
