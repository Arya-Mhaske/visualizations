{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A cell says 12 V on its label, yet a voltmeter across a working cell reads less. Draw more current and watch the voltage sag.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The emf is the energy the cell gives each coulomb. Some of that energy is spent pushing the charge through the cell's own internal resistance, so only ε − Ir is left at the terminals. With no current nothing is lost and V = ε. The more current you draw, the more is lost.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Make the load very large so the current is tiny. What is V close to?
  • Shrink the load and watch the point slide down the line. What happens to Ir?
  • Raise r. Why does the line fall faster?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The line's slope is −r, and its left end is the emf. You can drag the point along it.
