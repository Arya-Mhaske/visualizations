Walk away from it and see how much of their fields survives.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Far away, +q and −q are almost the same distance from you, so their two 1/r² fields nearly cancel. What survives is only the small difference, and that share shrinks like a/r. So the field is 1/r² times a/r, which is 1/r³. It falls faster than a single charge's field because the dipole has no net charge.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
