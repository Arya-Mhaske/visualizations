{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Potential is a plain number at each point, so potentials from different charges just add.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Unlike the field, potential has no direction, so there are no arrows to resolve. V = kq₁/r₁ + kq₂/r₂ + …, with signs. For a dipole (+q and −q close together) the two terms nearly cancel far away, which is why a dipole's potential falls faster than 1/r. On the line midway between equal and opposite charges V = 0.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Set the charges to +1 and −1. Put the probe at x = 0. What is V?
  • Set both to +1. Where is V smallest between them?
  • Move the probe far away for +1 and −1. How small does V get?
