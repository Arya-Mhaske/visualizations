{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Change the dielectric constant and see how much of the applied field the material cancels.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The dielectric makes its own field, opposite to E₀, with strength E₀(1 − 1/K). The net field is what is left, E₀/K. Water (K ≈ 80) cancels nearly all of it. A conductor would be the limit K → ∞, where the field inside is zero. K is 1 for vacuum, and is the factor by which the field and the voltage drop for fixed charge.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Set K = 1. How large is the opposing bar?
  • Set K to the value for paper (3.5). What fraction of E₀ is cancelled?
  • Set K = 80. How close is the net field to zero?

    NoteTypical values of K for paper, mica, glass and water are rounded. The value for water holds for slowly varying fields.
