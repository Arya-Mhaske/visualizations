{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block stage_class %}viz-stage--tall{% endblock %} {% block lead %}Two stations send signals together. A ship hears one later, and the delay puts it on a hyperbola.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Radio signals travel at the speed of light, 0.2998 km every microsecond. If a ship hears the signal from one station Δt microseconds after the other, it is cΔt km farther from one than the other. A fixed difference of distances is a hyperbola with the stations as foci. A second pair of stations gives a second hyperbola, and the ship is where they cross.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Drag the ship. What happens to the two time gaps?
  • Turn off the second pair. Can you tell where on the hyperbola the ship is?
  • Move the ship to the line halfway between two stations. What is the time gap?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note This is the idea behind the LORAN navigation system. The stations and distances here are made up for practice.
