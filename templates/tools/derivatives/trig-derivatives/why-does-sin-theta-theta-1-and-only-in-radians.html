In degrees the limit becomes π/180.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A thin sector of the unit circle is bigger than the triangle inside it (area ½ sin θ) and smaller than the triangle around it (½ tan θ). Its own area is ½ θ, but only when θ is measured in radians. Dividing out gives cos θ < sin θ/θ < 1, which squeezes to 1. In degrees the sector area is not ½θ, and the limit turns out to be π/180.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Switch to degrees. What does sin(θ°) ÷ θ settle at?
