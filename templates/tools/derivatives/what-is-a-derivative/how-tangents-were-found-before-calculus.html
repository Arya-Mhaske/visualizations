Try two methods, Fermat's and Barrow's, on the parabola y = x².{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Fermat looked for the "subtangent", the distance along the axis from the foot of the point to where the tangent meets the axis; for y = x² it is x/2, which fixes the tangent line. Barrow drew a tiny triangle on the curve whose sides are Δx and Δy and used its ratio as the slope, throwing away the tiny extra bits. Both ideas lead straight to the derivative.{% endblock %} {% block notes %}

These are simplified accounts of Fermat's method of adequality and Barrow's differential triangle. Historians describe their exact steps in different ways.
