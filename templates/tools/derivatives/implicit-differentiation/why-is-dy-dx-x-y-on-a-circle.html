{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Draw the radius and the tangent at a point on the circle. See how their slopes are linked.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius, and perpendicular slopes multiply to −1. The radius has slope y/x, so the tangent has slope −x/y. The same fact appears as x dx + y dy = 0: a step along the tangent has no component along the radius.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Slide the step along the tangent. Does x·dx + y·dy ever leave 0?
  • Change the radius. Does the product of the two slopes change?
  • Move to the top of the circle. What is the tangent slope there, and why?
