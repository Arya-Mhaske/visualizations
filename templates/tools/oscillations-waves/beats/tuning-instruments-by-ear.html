Press a fret, match it to the string above, and stop when the beats stop.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A tiny pitch gap is hard to judge, but it makes a slow throb you can time. A quarter of a beat a second at 440 Hz is a gap of only 0.06%. That is why tuners listen for beats. Each string is tuned to the one before, so leftover errors add up. That is why players finish by checking the low E against the high E.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Leave E₄ a little sharp, then match every other string to it. What does the final check say?

    Note This uses equal temperament, where one fret multiplies the pitch by 21/12. Working down from the fork is one common order; many players start from the A string. Real strings also sound harmonics, while these are pure tones. The low strings are quiet on phone speakers, so use earphones or watch the lobes.
