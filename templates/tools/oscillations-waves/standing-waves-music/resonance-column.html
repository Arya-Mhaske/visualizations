{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block stage_class %}viz-stage--tall{% endblock %} {% block lead %}Hold a tuning fork over a tube and change the water level. Find the two lengths where the tube sings loudest, then use them to find the speed of sound.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The water is a node and the open top is an antinode, so the first resonance is a quarter-wave and the next is three quarters. Both lengths are shorter than they look by the same end correction e, so l₂ − l₁ = λ/2 with e cancelled out. That gives v = νλ = 2ν(l₂ − l₁).{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Slide the water level slowly. Watch the coral dot on the curve. Record the two loudest lengths.
  • Compare v from 2ν(l₂ − l₁) with v from 4ν·l₁ alone. Which is closer to the true speed? Why?
  • Make the tube wider (bigger r). What happens to l₁, and to the difference l₂ − l₁?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The loudness curve is a model: the wave goes down, flips at the water, and comes back, keeping 80% of its size each round trip. The end correction e = 0.6 r is a standard rule of thumb for a tube like this. The speed of sound used is 331.3 √(1 + θ/273.15) m/s.
