{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Drop a shadow of the point onto a tilted line. The shadow is cos(A − B). Build it from two smaller shadows.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The point P is cos A across and sin A up. The line at angle B points along (cos B, sin B). Each step casts a shadow on that line: the across-step casts cos A cos B, the up-step casts sin A sin B. Together they reach the shadow of P. But that shadow is also the adjacent side of a right triangle with hypotenuse 1 and angle A − B, so it equals cos(A − B).{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Set B = 0°. The line is the x-axis. What is the shadow of the up-step?
  • Set A = B. The shadow of P is the whole radius. What do cos²A + sin²A add up to?
  • Make A − B = 90°. P's shadow vanishes. Which two shadows cancel?
