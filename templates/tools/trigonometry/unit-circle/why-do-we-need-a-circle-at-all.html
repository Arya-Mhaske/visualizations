{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A right triangle can only have angles below 90°. Turn the point past 90° and see what survives.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A right triangle already has a right angle, so its other angles must be less than 90°. Triangle ratios therefore stop there. A point on a circle has no such limit. It has an x and a y at every angle. For acute angles x ÷ r and y ÷ r are exactly the triangle ratios. So we keep those two fractions as the definition, and they keep working all the way round.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Set θ = 60°. Check that the triangle is there and that x ÷ r is the adjacent ÷ hypotenuse.
  • Push θ up to 90° and past it. When does the triangle disappear? What happens to x?
  • Go to 200°. Is there a triangle? Do x ÷ r and y ÷ r still make sense?
