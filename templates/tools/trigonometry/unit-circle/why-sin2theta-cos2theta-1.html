The two legs of the triangle change, but something about them does not.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The point (cos θ, sin θ) is on a circle of radius 1. The radius, the x-coordinate and the y-coordinate form a right triangle. Pythagoras says x² + y² = r², so cos²θ + sin²θ = 1. It holds at every angle, in every quadrant, because squaring removes the signs and the radius never changes.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Slide θ through 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°. What are cos²θ and sin²θ at each?
  • Set θ = 45°. How much of the bar is cos²θ?
  • Go into the third quadrant, where both are negative. Does the sum still reach 1?
