Which quantity stays fixed, the angles or something else?{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The two angles change from ray to ray, and so does their ratio α ÷ β. What stays the same is sin α ÷ sin β. It depends only on the two materials. That constant is the refractive index n, and the rule is Snell's law. It is also why the plot of sin β against sin α is a straight line through the origin.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • In water, try α = 20°, 40° and 60°. Does α ÷ β stay the same? Does sin α ÷ sin β?
  • Switch to diamond. Does the ray bend more or less towards the normal?
  • Set α near 85°. What is the largest β you can get in water? Why does it stop well short of 90°?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The indices are for yellow light: water 1.33, glass 1.5, diamond 2.42, with air taken as 1. The rule is a law found by experiment. Later it can be explained by light travelling at different speeds in different materials.
