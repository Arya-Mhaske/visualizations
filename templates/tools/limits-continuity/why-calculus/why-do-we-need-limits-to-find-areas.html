{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Fill a circle with polygons of 6, 12, 24 … sides and watch the areas creep towards π.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The area of a polygon with straight sides can be found exactly. A circle has none, so Archimedes squeezed it between two polygons, one inside and one outside. Doubling the number of sides narrows the gap each time. The areas get as close to π as you like, but in any finite number of steps they never equal it. The number they approach is the limit.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Go to 1536 sides. Is the inner polygon area equal to π yet?
