{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block stage_class %}viz-stage--flow{% endblock %} {% block lead %}Substitute one linear system into another and see where the product matrix comes from.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}If x and y depend on u and v, and u and v depend on s and t, then x and y depend on s and t. Putting the second set of equations into the first, each coefficient of s or t is a row of the first matrix times a column of the second. Cayley defined the product of matrices this way, so that combining two substitutions is multiplying their matrices.{% endblock %} {% block notes %}
Note Arthur Cayley (1858) is usually credited with defining matrix multiplication to fit composition of linear substitutions. The motive described here is the standard account.
