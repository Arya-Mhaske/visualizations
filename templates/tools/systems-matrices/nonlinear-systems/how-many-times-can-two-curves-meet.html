{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Move a line across a circle, or two conics over each other, and count the meeting points.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A line and a circle give a quadratic equation, which has 0, 1 or 2 solutions: the line misses, touches, or cuts the circle. Two conics have degree 2 each, so they can meet in at most 2 × 2 = 4 points. This is a case of a theorem of Bézout. Fewer meetings are possible, and so is the full 4.{% endblock %} {% block notes %}
Note The 'at most 4' is Bézout's theorem for two curves of degree 2. It counts points in the complex plane too, so some of them may not be visible on the graph.
