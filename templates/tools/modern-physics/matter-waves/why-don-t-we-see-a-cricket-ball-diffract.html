Diffraction needs the gap to be about as wide as the wavelength.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}The first dark angle satisfies sin θ = λ/a. When λ ≪ a the spread is far too small to see. A cricket ball has λ near 10⁻³⁴ m, so no gap you could build is narrow enough. An electron through a gap the size of an atom spreads out widely.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Choose the electron and set the gap near 10⁻⁹ m. What does the pattern look like?
  • Widen the gap to 1 mm. What happens to the pattern?
  • Choose the cricket ball. What gap would you need to see any spread, and does such a gap exist?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note The pattern is the single-slit one, sin²(x)/x² with x = πa sin θ/λ. For the cricket ball the true spread is thinner than a pen line, so the picture is drawn wider than the truth.
