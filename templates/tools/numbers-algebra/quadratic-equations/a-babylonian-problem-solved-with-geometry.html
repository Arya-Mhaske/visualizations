{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Cut a strip off a rectangle and move it, and a quadratic solves itself.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}If the long side of a rectangle is p more than the short side, cutting a strip of width p/2 off the long end and moving it to the top nearly makes a square. Only a small corner, (p/2)², is missing. So m² = q + (p/2)², where m is the average of the two sides. This is exactly completing the square.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Use p = 4 and q = 45. What are L and W?
  • Change p to 2. How does the corner change?
  • Choose a q so that m comes out whole. Is L whole too?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note Babylonian clay tablets from about 1800 BC solve problems of this kind with a step-by-step recipe that matches this cut-and-move. Whether the scribes thought of it as a picture is a modern interpretation, and historians do not all agree.
