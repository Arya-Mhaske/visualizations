For some c it needs the square root of a negative number on the way to a real answer.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}In 1572 Bombelli saw that x³ = 15x + 4 has the real root 4, yet Cardano's formula asks for √−121 on the way. He treated √−121 as a number, 11i, and the imaginary parts cancelled: (2 + i) + (2 − i) = 4. Whenever the line cuts the curve three times, the formula passes through a negative number under the root. So imaginary numbers are not decoration. They are the road to real answers.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
