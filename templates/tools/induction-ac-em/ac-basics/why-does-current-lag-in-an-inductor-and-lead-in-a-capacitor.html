Try a phase for the current, and see which one obeys the rule.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}A capacitor's voltage is a pile of charge, so charge must arrive first: i is largest while v climbs fastest, and i leads by 90°. An inductor is the reverse. Voltage sets how fast the current changes, so i keeps rising until v falls to zero, and i lags by 90°. A resistor has no pile to build, so v and i stay in step.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • On the inductor, find the phase that puts the dashed curve on v.
  • Scrub time to where v is largest. How steep is the purple slope of i there?
  • Now find the phase for the capacitor. Why is it the opposite sign?
  • Try the resistor. Which phase works, and why is there only one?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    Note "Leads" does not mean the current sees the future. The pattern has been running for many cycles, and each peak of i arrives a quarter cycle before the next peak of v.
