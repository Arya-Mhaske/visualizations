{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}A Carnot engine needs heat to flow with no temperature gap, which takes forever. Add a gap and friction and watch what you lose.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Heat only flows across a temperature gap, and the bigger the gap the faster it flows. So an engine that runs at any useful speed must work with its gas a little cooler than the hot source and a little hotter than the cold sink. That already lowers the efficiency. Friction wastes more work. Only a gap of zero and no friction reaches the Carnot limit, and then the engine delivers no power.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}

  • Slide δ up. Where is the power greatest?
  • Add friction. What happens to the efficiency curve?
