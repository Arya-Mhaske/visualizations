{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}The same sunshine falls on land and sea. Compare how much each warms, and which way the air moves.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Water needs far more heat to warm by one degree than soil does (specific heat 4186 against about 800 J/kg·K, and sea water mixes deep). So land warms quickly by day and the sea barely changes. Warm air rises over the land and cooler air blows in from the sea: a sea breeze. At night the land cools faster, and the breeze reverses.{% endblock %} {% block notes %}

The sunshine (400 W/m² by day, 100 W/m² lost at night) and the layer depths are round numbers for a model, not measurements.
