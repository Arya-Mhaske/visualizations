{% extends "tools/base_tool.html" %} {% block lead %}Hot tea cools quickly at first, then more and more slowly. Move the tangent along the curve.{% endblock %} {% block why_text %}Newton's law says the cooling rate is proportional to how much hotter the tea is than the room. As the gap shrinks, so does the rate, so the curve flattens. A rate that is proportional to what is left gives an exponential: T = T_s + (T₀ − T_s)e^(−kt). It never quite reaches room temperature.{% endblock %} {% block try_items %}
  • Slide t. Where is the tangent steepest, and where is it nearly flat?
  • Raise k. What happens to the curve and the dashed line?
  • Make the room hotter. Does the tea still cool to room temperature?
    • {% endblock %} {% block notes %}
    NoteNewton's law holds well only for modest temperature gaps. For a very hot object radiation makes it cool faster than this.
